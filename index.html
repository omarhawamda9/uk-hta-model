Methodology & Governance

Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio

The ICER is the additional cost of a new technology per additional quality-adjusted life year gained over its comparator: ICER = ΔCost / ΔQALY. Here ΔCost is the net price after any discount minus the comparator cost, and ΔQALY is incremental life years multiplied by the annual utility gain. Where ΔCost is zero or negative, the technology dominates.

Confidential Patient Access Schemes

A Patient Access Scheme is a commercial arrangement between a manufacturer and NHS England that lowers the effective price paid by the NHS without changing the published list price. The discount is typically confidential, which is why the model reports a break-even discount rather than a fixed price.

NICE Severity Weighting

NICE applies a standard threshold of £20,000 to £30,000 per QALY. Under the severity modifier, QALY weights of 1.2x and 1.7x apply to conditions with greater absolute or proportional shortfall, lifting the effective threshold to about £36,000 and £51,000. Highly Specialised Technologies for very rare conditions are assessed against a ceiling of up to £100,000 per QALY.

NHS England £20m Budget Impact Test

Where the expected net annual cost to the NHS in any of the first three years exceeds £20 million, NHS England may negotiate commercial terms or phase the introduction. The model compares net price multiplied by treated patients against this limit.

This tool is an educational simplification and is not affiliated with or endorsed by NICE or NHS England. It omits discounting, time horizon, comparator mix, uncertainty analysis and decision modifiers beyond severity, and must not be used for reimbursement decisions or investment advice.
